# General solutions for noise from Eurorack USB audio interfaces (USB ground loops, isolators, alternative fixes, software filtering)

Scope note: Several primary forum threads specific to Eurorack USB interfaces (ModWiggler "ES-9 hum on all inputs - grounding issue?", "Noise/Hum - Expert Sleepers ES9", "ES-9 Noise and Volume Issue", "What is causing the digital noise in my eurorack system?", and Gearspace "USB ground loop isolators anyone use them" / "Ground loop problem solved – Intona praise") were found in search but could NOT be fetched (site errors). Their existence is evidence that the problem is common, but their content and outcomes are unverified here. Most of the cited evidence therefore comes from adjacent gear (Elektron, Sequential, desktop synths, USB DACs), which uses the same USB-ground mechanism.

## (a) Do USB ground loop isolators actually help with Eurorack USB audio interface noise?

### Takeaway
They help when the noise travels through the USB ground or data lines (typical signs: noise appears or disappears when the USB cable is plugged or unplugged, or changes with computer activity, or goes away with the laptop on battery). They do not help with noise from analog cabling, the Eurorack PSU or bus, RF, or other ground paths such as HDMI or the audio outputs into a grounded mixer. For a USB 2.0 High-Speed interface such as the ES-8 or ES-9, only a High-Speed isolator will work. Cheap ADuM3160 boards (Full Speed only) won't. Reports from users are mixed: many say it was "solved", some saw "little difference".

### Cited Findings
- USB isolators break the electrical ground between computer and USB audio device while passing data. They are likely to help when noise appears when the USB cable is connected, changes with computer activity (mouse, CPU, GPU, charger noise), or stops when a laptop runs on battery — [Hifimediy: Fix USB Audio Noise](https://hifimediy.com/usb-audio-noise/)
- The same source says isolation won't fix noise from bad analog cables, amp or mic-gain noise, defective equipment, RF interference, HDMI ground loops, or noise entering via another connection. Its suggested test: if the noise clearly changes when the USB cable is plugged or removed, start with isolation — [Hifimediy](https://hifimediy.com/usb-audio-noise/)
- Elektronauts "Non-isolated USB sourced noise" thread: USB isolators "done the trick" for one user's Minitaur and Blofeld, and another user reported the same for a Minitaur. A third user tried several isolators and saw little difference. Pulling USB removed the noise spikes, which confirmed USB as the source. Balanced cables made no difference in one case — [Elektronauts thread 132892](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/non-isolated-usb-sourced-noise-on-various-synths-definitely-seems-to-be-a-thing/132892)
- Elektronauts "USB noise" thread: an iFi iDefender+ reportedly made "all digital noise issues … gone forever" for one user. An ARCELI isolator module worked for M8 tracker noise. ADuM3160 isolators did NOT work for a Dreadbox Typhon, likely because of insufficient current, while a Dreadbox USB power/data splitter solved it. A StarTech powered hub did not help. In one case the noise persisted even with the modular powered off, so the isolator could not have been expected to fix it — [Elektronauts thread 192813](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/usb-noise/192813)
- One user claimed that Elektron devices use galvanic isolator chips on their USB port while cheaper devices often skip them. This is a user explanation, not verified — [Elektronauts 132892](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/non-isolated-usb-sourced-noise-on-various-synths-definitely-seems-to-be-a-thing/132892)
- Archimago's measurement of the Topping HS01: with a DAC on a PC, the J-Test showed high noise and sidebands. With the HS01 in line, results matched a clean Raspberry Pi 4 source, 1 kHz THD+N and the noise floor improved, and the 8 kHz USB PHY noise was controlled. With a balanced DAC the isolator made no difference, and he heard no difference in a room without ground-loop problems — [Archimago: Topping HS01 measurements](https://archimago.blogspot.com/2022/03/measurements-topping-hs01-usb-20.html?m=0)
- Sound On Sound (HX Stomp Q&A): USB creates a second ground path alongside mains earth. Desktops are usually earthed and close the loop, while laptops often aren't. The suggested fixes are a USB isolator (1.1 and 2.0 versions exist and quality varies by brand) or an audio line isolator such as the ART DTI — [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/sound-advice/q-why-does-my-hx-stomp-pedal-hum-if-connected-computer-running-its-editing-software?amp)
- Eurorack-specific product: the Manikin Electronic "Modul 2" is a 4 HP USB 2.0 High-Speed (480 Mbit/s) isolator module with USB-B host in and USB-A device out, 5 V/400 mA max output, €179 incl. VAT at Schneidersladen. It is marketed against ground loops that cause crackling and dropouts in Eurorack USB setups — [Schneidersladen](https://schneidersladen.de/en/manikin-electronic-modul-2-usb-high-speed-isolator)
- ES-9 is "class-compliant USB 2.0" (USB-C), 16 in/16 out at up to 96 kHz/24-bit. The product page says nothing about grounding or isolation — [Expert Sleepers ES-9](https://expert-sleepers.co.uk/es9.html)

### Inferences
- A 16x16 channel, 96 kHz class-compliant interface like the ES-9 (and likely the ES-8, 12 in/8 out at up to 96 kHz) needs USB High Speed bandwidth. A Full Speed ADuM3160 isolator will either force a mode the device doesn't support or fail to enumerate. Only HS isolators (ADuM4165/ISOUSB211/Intona/HS01/Hifime ISO3/Manikin) are candidates. The same logic likely applies to other multichannel UAC2 Eurorack devices. Check each device's USB speed: some smaller modules may be Full Speed UAC1, where an ADuM3160 board would work.
- An isolator only removes the USB leg of a loop. In Eurorack the case PSU is mains-earthed and the audio outs often go to a mains-earthed mixer, interface or monitors, so after isolating USB the remaining loop may still exist through the audio cables.

### Gaps
- No ModWiggler, Expert Sleepers forum or Reddit r/modular thread with a verified outcome for an isolator on an ES-8/ES-9/Bitbox/Disting could be fetched (ModWiggler and Gearspace returned client errors, Reddit had no results). Eurorack-specific success or failure rates remain unverified.
- No Expert Sleepers manual or FAQ statement on isolators was found.

## (b) How USB isolators work, the models available, prices and technical limits

### Takeaway
They use digital isolators (magnetic/capacitive coupling) on D+/D- plus an isolated DC/DC converter for VBUS, so the host and device grounds no longer connect. The key split is Full Speed only (ADuM3160/ADuM4160, around €10–35) versus High Speed 480 Mbit/s (ADuM4165/ADuM3165, TI ISOUSB211, Intona, Topping HS01, Hifime ISO3, roughly €60–400). Other limits are current (often 350–900 mA) and possible incompatibility with Full Speed or USB 1.1 devices on some HS units. Isochronous/UAC2 support is implied by vendors (they sell for 24/192+ and multichannel DACs) but not stated in chip datasheets.

### Cited Findings
- ADuM3160: low speed (1.5 Mbps) and full speed (12 Mbps) only, 2.5 kV rms UL 1577. Its page doesn't claim High Speed support — [Analog Devices ADuM3160](https://www.analog.com/ADuM3160)
- ADuM4165: USB 2.0 low/full/high speed (480 Mbps) with automatic speed detection, redriving and high-speed data retiming, 5.7 kV rms (UL 1577), 50 kV/µs CMTI, and 4.5–5.5 V or 3.0–3.6 V supplies per side. It needs a companion isolated power part (ADuM6028 listed). The page doesn't mention audio or isochronous transfers — [Analog Devices ADuM4165](https://www.analog.com/en/products/adum4165.html)
- TI ISOUSB211 is an isolated USB 2.0 compliant repeater supporting 480 Mbps that redrives the signal across the barrier. TI notes that audio transformers can isolate analog but not digital audio. Its application note is aimed at ground-loop hiss, whine or hum and doesn't discuss isochronous transfers — [TI SLLA601](https://ti.com/lit/pdf/slla601)
- Olimex USB-ISO-HS: TI ISOUSB211, low/full/high speed auto-detect, up to 350 mA to the device. An optional external supply must itself be isolated. Price AUD 74.40 incl. GST (about €45). It's described as necessary for DACs above 96/24 or multichannel — [Core Electronics: Olimex USB-ISO-HS](https://core-electronics.com.au/usb-high-speed-isolator-galvanic-protection.html.md)
- Hifimediy comparison table (USD): Hifime Full-Speed ISO2 $29 (also a high-current version at $35), Hifime Hi-Speed ISO3 v2 $59 (up to 500 mA), Intona 7054-DK $229, 7055-B $349 (HS, 5 kV), 7055-C $359 (adds SuperSpeed), 7055-D $499. Product listings show higher prices (7054 $299, 7055-B and 7055-C $389, 7055-D $549). A 192 kHz-capable sound card may not work in Full Speed mode even at lower sample rates, isolation voltage "normally not important" for audio ground loops, and some interfaces may still need external power — [Hifimediy: Choosing the right USB isolator](https://hifimediy.com/2020/06/choosing-the-right-usb-isolator/)
- Intona 7055-C at Batronix (DE): €391.51 incl. 19% VAT. USB 2.0 HS and USB 3.0 SS, 1 kV DC isolation, up to 900 mA on the isolated side, or 2 A with an auxiliary micro-USB supply — [Batronix: Intona 7055-C](https://www.batronix.com/shop/USB-isolators/Intona-7055-C.html)
- Topping HS01: USB 2.0 HS (480 Mbps), works at UAC2 rates (tested at 24/192), 1 kV rms isolation on data and power, about US$70. It is NOT compatible with USB 1.0/1.1 devices. Readers reported a UAC1 DAC not being recognised and an ADC glitching through it (unverified reader reports) — [Archimago](https://archimago.blogspot.com/2022/03/measurements-topping-hs01-usb-20.html?m=0)
- iFi iDefender+: not a data isolator. It "intelligently" disconnects the computer's ground when it detects a loop. A USB-C aux power input blocks host VBUS and powers the device from an external supply. About US$59 — [Bloom Audio: iFi iDefender+](https://bloomaudio.com/products/ifi-idefender)
- Manikin Modul 2 (Eurorack, HS, 400 mA): €179 — [Schneidersladen](https://schneidersladen.de/en/manikin-electronic-modul-2-usb-high-speed-isolator)
- Generic ADuM3160/ADuM4160 boards are sold on eBay and marketplaces as "12 Mbps and 1.5 Mbps" isolators — [Fruugo listing](https://www.fruugo.dk/usb-to-usb-digital-o-signal-isolator-module-with-adum3160-chip-supports-12mbps-and-15mbps-data-transfer-speeds/p-431667124-906655634); [eBay listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/232469730087)
- Current limits matter in practice: the ADuM3160 isolators failed on a Dreadbox Typhon "likely due to insufficient current" — [Elektronauts 192813](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/usb-noise/192813)
- A secondhand claim says low-speed isolator versions "don't work with Focusrite gear" — [MOD forum](https://forum.mod.audio/t/usb-isolator/4702)

### Inferences
- For Eurorack modules powered from the case rails (ES-8/ES-9 take ±12 V from the bus), the VBUS current limit of the isolator is less of an issue than for bus-powered desktop synths, since the module probably draws little or nothing from VBUS. The bigger constraint is HS support.
- HS isolators that retime/redrive (ADuM4165, ISOUSB211) are transparent at the packet level, so isochronous UAC2 streams should pass, consistent with HS01 and Hifime claims for 192 kHz/DSD. Vendors don't spell out isochronous support.
- Ranking for an ES-8/ES-9-class device in the EU: Hifime ISO3 (about €55–60) or Olimex USB-ISO-HS (about €45–60) as the budget HS choice; Topping HS01 (about €70–90); Manikin Modul 2 (€179) to keep it in the rack; Intona 7054/7055 (€250–400) for lab grade. Avoid ADuM3160 boards for HS devices.

- ADuM3165 specs were not fetched (only ADuM4165 confirmed). It is believed to be a lower-isolation sibling with High Speed support (unverified).
- Douk/Nobsound HS isolators were not researched.

## (c) Alternative hardware fixes and diagnosis

### Takeaway
Before buying anything, isolate the path. Test with USB unplugged, then with the laptop on battery, then with everything on one power strip, then with the audio output path ground-lifted. The cheapest effective fixes are often: everything on one power strip or outlet; laptop on battery or a charger without earth; breaking the audio-side loop with a DI box with ground lift, a transformer isolator or balanced outputs; and splitting USB data from power. A powered hub alone is not a reliable fix. Tones at 1 kHz, 8 kHz and harmonics point to USB packet activity, not mains hum (50 Hz plus harmonics).

### Cited Findings
- Sequential/DSI USB ground-loop guide, in order: unplug the laptop from the wall to test; put the synth and computer on the same outlet; add a surge protector or power conditioner; wall-power bus-powered interfaces; try another USB port; try a different or shielded cable; try a USB hub. Then: a DIY ground lift by cutting the shield and the GND wire of a USB cable (risky, not recommended here), or a commercial USB isolator — [Sequential forum](https://forum.sequential.com/index.php/topic,1599.msg17847.html)
- Separate power strip or outlet for the USB device fixed a MicroMonsta 2 whine. A cheap DI box with ground lift (Pyle, about $11) reduced noise but didn't eliminate it. Audio ground-loop isolators reduced the noise but colored the sound; one user said only Radial units tested clean. Dedicated wall adapters instead of USB power helped others — [Elektronauts 192813](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/usb-noise/192813)
- Ferrite rings worked for one user "even with USB". Switching from USB to DIN MIDI solved one user's problem — [Elektronauts 132892](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/non-isolated-usb-sourced-noise-on-various-synths-definitely-seems-to-be-a-thing/132892)
- 8 kHz USB noise: the tone matches the USB 2.0 microframe rate (125 µs, so 8000/s), with peaks at 8, 16 and 24 kHz on FFT. A bad hub also raised the broadband floor. A powered hub alone gave about 1 dB of improvement; isolating the data lines with an optical USB cable gave about 15–17 dB. Hub quality varies, and a cheap hub made things worse. The tone was only audible or measurable on a sensitive unbalanced input — [Archimago: USB hubs and 8 kHz PHY noise](https://archimago.blogspot.com/2015/05/measurements-usb-hubs-and-8khz-phy.html?m=0)
- Sound On Sound ground-loop principles: never remove safety earth, since signal screens can't carry fault current. Prefer balanced wiring. Use a roughly 100 Ω resistor in the screen at one end, ground-lift leads or built-in lifts, nylon rack hardware to avoid case-to-case contact, and add gear one piece at a time while testing — [Sound On Sound: Ground loops explained](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/ground-loops-explained)
- Audio line isolators (ART DTI, CleanBox II) between device output and amp break the analog loop. Keep isolation transformers away from mains transformers to avoid magnetic hum — [Sound On Sound HX Stomp Q&A](https://www.soundonsound.com/sound-advice/q-why-does-my-hx-stomp-pedal-hum-if-connected-computer-running-its-editing-software?amp)
- Eurorack output isolation modules: the Happy Nerding Isolator (transformer-isolated balanced TRS outs) is recommended on ModularGrid for isolating the modular from the audio interface. The Joranalogue Transmit 2 claims galvanic isolation via solid-state parts — [ModularGrid forum](https://modulargrid.net/e/forum/posts/index/11449)
- Loopop's "12 ways to fix noise" (summarised by Sonicstate) covers USB whine, split data/power, USB isolators, ferrite chokes, isolated power, IEC isolators, "one strip", no daisy chains, audio isolators, DI boxes, balanced gear, notch filters and "advanced algorithms" — [Sonicstate](https://sonicstate.com/news/2025/03/25/better-ways-to-fix-noise-hum-buzz-more-/)
- iFi iDefender+ with an external PSU combines ground lift with clean VBUS, about $59 — [Bloom Audio](https://bloomaudio.com/products/ifi-idefender)

### Inferences (diagnosis procedure for a Eurorack USB interface)
1. Record the noise floor with inputs patched to nothing or shorted (a dummy cable), outputs at a known gain. Look at an FFT (REW, Audacity Plot Spectrum, SPAN). 50/100/150 Hz combs suggest a mains ground loop. Lines at 1 kHz (Full Speed SOF), 8 kHz (HS microframes) and their multiples suggest USB bus activity. Lines that move with CPU or GPU load or mouse movement suggest computer switching noise. A whine that changes with patch or DSP load in the module suggests internal digital, power-rail or clock coupling, which isolators can't fix.
2. Unplug USB while monitoring the module's analog path (if usable standalone). If the noise disappears, the cause is USB-related.
3. Laptop on battery with the charger unplugged. If the noise disappears, the cause is the charger or earth path: fix with an isolator, an iDefender, a non-earthed charger, or the same strip.
4. Put the case PSU, computer, interface or mixer and monitors on one power strip.
5. Lift or isolate the audio output path (balanced outs, DI ground lift, transformer isolator). If the noise persists with USB isolated, the loop is through the audio cables.
6. Swap the case PSU or test the module in a different case or with a different PSU, to rule out bus noise from the PSU itself (switch-mode PSUs, overloaded rails).
7. Ferrite on the USB cable for HF and RF content; short shielded cable; avoid USB-C dongles or hubs and plug directly into a host port.
- USB SOF timing: Full Speed frames are 1 ms (1 kHz) and High Speed microframes 125 µs (8 kHz). This is standard USB 2.0 timing; the 8 kHz part is confirmed by Archimago, the 1 kHz figure is from the USB spec, not fetched here.

## (d) Can narrowband noise be removed in software?

### Takeaway
Yes, if the noise is stable and narrowband (a fixed tone plus harmonics). Notch filters, comb filters, or a harmonic de-hummer remove it with little collateral damage, either in the DAW or inside an OWL-platform module (C++, FAUST, Pd/Heavy, Gen, SOUL). It works poorly if the tone drifts with load: fixed notches miss it, and you need adaptive tracking (RX De-hum adaptive, or an LMS adaptive notch). Broadband hiss or crackle needs a spectral denoiser and leaves artifacts. Software only treats the recording, while CV-carrying signals and live monitoring stay noisy, so the hardware fix is preferable.

### Cited Findings
- iZotope RX De-hum: base frequency 50/60 Hz or "Free" manual setting, up to 7 harmonics with slope and odd/even linking, adjustable Filter Q, and Learn from a hum-only section. Adaptive mode re-analyzes over a learning time for evolving hum. Linear-phase FIR mode adds latency and pre-ringing; minimum-phase IIR only post-rings. iZotope suggests De-click for very high-frequency buzz. The page doesn't state a maximum base frequency — [iZotope De-hum](https://help.izotope.com/docs/rx-plug-in-pack/De-hum.html)
- RX De-hum after the mixer helped for USB-synth whine and seemed fairly transparent — [Elektronauts 192813](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/usb-noise/192813)
- ReaFIR (free with REAPER) Subtract mode: build a noise profile automatically from a noise-only section, or draw it. Larger FFT sizes are more precise, so you can build the profile at a large FFT size and switch back. Raising the profile adds artifacts. FFT mode can alias; the "reduce artifacts" option switches to a linear FIR with limited effectiveness — [Cockos ReaFIR wiki](https://www.cockos.com/wiki/index.php/ReaFIR)
- Bertom Denoiser v3: Classic is free or pay-what-you-want; Pro ($25) adds per-band thresholds and an adaptive mode — [KVR news](https://www.kvraudio.com/news-print.php?id=56989)
- OWL platform (Rebel Technology; also used by Befaco Lich): OwlProgram builds patches from C++, FAUST (`make FAUST=…`, controls mapped via `[OWL:A]` metadata), Pure Data via Heavy/hvcc (`make HEAVY=…`), Max Gen, SOUL and Maximilian. Targets include `load` (run from RAM), `store` (flash), `web` and `native` — [GitHub RebelTechnology/OwlProgram](https://github.com/RebelTechnology/OwlProgram)
- FAUST filters library provides `fi.notchw(width,freq)` (biquad notch, width in Hz) and feed-forward and feedback combs (`ff_comb`, `ff_fcomb`, `fb_comb`, `fb_fcomb` with fractional delay). A feed-forward comb `y = x − x[n−N]` places nulls at multiples of SR/N — [FAUST filters.lib](https://faustlibraries.grame.fr/libs/filters/)
- Loopop's noise video includes chapters on "Notch filters" and "Advanced algorithms" as last-resort software fixes — [Sonicstate](https://sonicstate.com/news/2025/03/25/better-ways-to-fix-noise-hum-buzz-more-/)

### Inferences (tradeoffs)
- Fixed notch bank (biquads at f0, 2f0 … nf0): cheap on a Cortex-M4/M7 (one biquad is about 5 MACs per sample per notch), zero latency, transparent with narrow Q. It fails if f0 drifts more than the notch width, so the Q must be widened, which costs more signal. For 8 kHz plus harmonics at 48 kHz SR there are only 2 harmonics below Nyquist (8 and 16 kHz). At 96 kHz there are 5.
- Feed-forward comb: one delay line nulls all harmonics of SR/N at once. That only fits when f0 divides the sample rate exactly (8 kHz at 48 kHz gives N = 6; 1 kHz gives N = 48). It also creates nulls at DC and dips in between, which colors audio heavily; suitable for diagnostics but not as a transparent fix. A notch-comb variant (feedback with pole radius near 1) is narrower.
- Adaptive approaches: an LMS adaptive notch or a PLL-tracking notch follows drifting tones, and RX De-hum adaptive does the same offline or in plugin form. Adaptive noise cancellation with a reference input works only if a clean reference of the noise exists, which is rarely the case here.
- Spectral subtraction (ReaFIR, Bertom, RX Spectral De-noise) handles broadband or irregular digital noise but introduces "musical noise" artifacts. It suits recordings, not live CV paths.
- In-module firmware filtering only helps if the noise enters before the module's ADC or DSP. If the noise is added at the DAC output stage or on the output cables or ground, firmware can't remove it. Notching also damages CV and audio content at those frequencies.
- If the noise is from the computer side (USB audio stream), the DAW plugin filters the input; if it is on the module's outputs to an external mixer, only firmware or hardware can help.
